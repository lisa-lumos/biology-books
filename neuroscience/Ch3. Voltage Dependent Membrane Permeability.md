# Ch3. Voltage-Dependent Membrane Permeability
## Overview
1. Resting state
   - Inside: about -70 mV
   - Na+ concentration is high outside, K+ concentration is high inside.
   - Voltage-gated Na+ and K+ channels are closed.
2. Depolarization begins
   - A neighboring section of the axon has already fired an action potential. 
   - Positive charge from that region flows inside the axon into the next region. 
   - This makes the next patch less negative. When it reaches the threshold (about -55 mV), Voltage-gated Na+ channels open.
3. Na+ rushes in
   - Now Na+ rapidly enters because Na+ concentration is much higher outside, and the inside is still relatively negative.
   - The membrane potential quickly changes: -70 mV -> -55 mV (threshold) -> 0 mV -> about +30 mV
   - This rapid rise is the action potential.
4. K+ channels open
   - A fraction of a millisecond later: Na+ channels become inactivated. Voltage-gated K+ channels open.
   - K+ leaves the cell because K+ concentration is higher inside.
   - Repolarization. As positive K+ leaves, the inside becomes negative again.
5. Hyperpolarization
   - K+ channels close slowly.
   - Too much K+ leaves, so the membrane briefly becomes even more negative than resting (around -80 mV).
   - Then the channels close.
   - Leak channels and the Na+/K+ pump gradually restore the resting conditions.

A - B - C
- Section A fires.
- Na+ enters A, making its inside positive.
- That positive charge spreads inside the axon into section B.
- B reaches threshold and fires.
- B sends positive current into C.
- The process repeats.

The region that just fired has its Na+ channels in an inactivated state. During this brief period, they cannot reopen. So when section B fires, it cannot reactivate section A. The signal can only activate section C, causing the action potential to travel in one direction along the axon.
