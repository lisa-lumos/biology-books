# Ch2. Electrical Signals of Nerve Cells
## Overview
Resting membrane potential. The constant negative potential that neurons have at rest. 

Action potential. Transient positive transmembrane potential. Responsible for long-range transmission of info, within the nervous system, and from the nervous system to its target organs, such as a muscle. Typically ~1ms. 

These electrical signals arise from ion fluxes, brought about by the selective ionic permeability of nerve cell membranes, produced by ion channels, and the non-uniform distribution of these ions across the membrane, crated by active transporters. 

## Electrical Signals of Nerve Cells
Receptor potentials. Due to the activation of sensory neurons by external stimuli, such as light/sound/heat. Amplitudes are graded in proportion to the magnitude of the sensory stimulus. 

Synaptic potentials. Associated with communication between neurons, at synaptic contacts. Amplitudes vary according to the number/strength of synapses activated, and the previous amount of synaptic activity. 

Depolarizations that cause the membrane potential to meet/exceed threshold evoke action potentials. 

A way to elicit action potential:Inserting a micro-electrode into a neuron, then connecting the electrode to a battery. 

The amplitude of the action potential is independent of the magnitude of the current used to invoke it - larger currents do not elicit larger action potentials. So the action potentials of a given neuron are all-or-none - they occur fully, or not at all. 

If the amplitude/duration of the stimulus current is increased sufficiently, multiple action potentials occur. aka, the intensity of a stimulus is encoded in the freqency of action potentials, rather than in their amplitude.
